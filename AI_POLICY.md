# AI-Assisted Code Development Policy

## Our approach to AI use

We encourage thoughtful use of AI tools, including agentic coding, to explore ideas, improve productivity, and strengthen software quality. AI tool use is optional. Contributions are evaluated on quality and value, regardless of the tools used.

Use AI to support your judgment not replace. Authors remain responsible for their contributions, including explaining their changes and helping address problems those changes cause.

Questions, requests for help, and early design discussions are welcome, even without a polished pull request.

This policy covers code, tests, documentation, issue reports, and discussion responses.

## Avoid extractive contributions

An **extractive contribution**[^eghbal] costs the project more effort to review, correct, or maintain than the value it adds. Consider the time your contribution will take to review and maintain.

## Make pull requests reviewable

Authors can seek early feedback or review for merge. State which kind of review you want in the description so reviewers know what to expect.

- **Exploratory review:** Say what is uncertain and what feedback you want. You do not need to understand every detail yet.
- **Review for merge:** Before requesting review, authors should:
  - Read the complete diff and understand the changes well enough to discuss and revise them.
  - Keep the pull request focused and manageable, with a clear description.
  - Split unrelated changes into separate pull requests when practical.
  - Keep formatting changes to a minimum.

## Issue reports and discussion

AI can help draft issue reports and replies, but the final message must reflect your own intent and judgment.

Personally review AI-generated issue reports and replies before posting. Check factual claims, ground assessments of severity and cause in evidence, and label unverified claims. In issue reports, state whether you reproduced the behavior.

**Do not configure agents to respond autonomously on pull requests or issues.**

## Enforcement

Maintainers may require revisions or close impractical submissions. Repeated violations may result in a ban. Before escalating to a ban, maintainers will normally give contributors a reasonable opportunity to improve.

[^eghbal]: Adapted from Nadia Eghbal's discussion of extractive contributions in *Working in Public: The Making and Maintenance of Open Source Software* (Stripe Press, 2020).
