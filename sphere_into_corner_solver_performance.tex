\section*{Sphere-into-Corner Solver Performance}

This report summarizes the unified current-code, output-enabled
sphere-into-corner response matrix run on \texttt{rzwhippet53} on 2026-08-17.
The authoritative full-output matrix is
\path{build-rzwhippet-toss_4_x86_64_ib-llvm@19.1.3-release/paper_sphere_into_corner_response_20260817_105859}.
Commands, logs, response histories, executable and source identity, status,
validation, plots, and selected field renders are archived under
\path{/usr/workspace/tupek2/dev/buckling_solver_paper/results/sphere_into_corner/solver_response_20260817_105859}.

\section*{Configuration}

The three-dimensional model uses the first-order
\path{hollow_sphere_octant_corner.g} mesh.  It is a hollow spherical-octant
shell with no essential displacement boundary conditions.  The material is
Neo-Hookean with density 1, Young's modulus 6, and Poisson ratio 0.33.  A load
patch is selected from the upper 24\% of the outer-surface projection range in
the direction opposite $(-1,-1,-1)$.  A traction drives this patch toward the
three coordinate planes over 16 equal increments from time zero to one.  The
peak traction magnitude is 0.019.

The coordinate planes are represented by a custom smoothed penalty boundary
integral on the outer surface.  The penalty parameter is 8, the nominal element
size is 0.025, and the smoothing band is $2.5\times10^{-5}$.  This is not a
Tribol mortar-contact calculation.  Before penalty contact constrains the
shell, the quasistatic tangent is intentionally under-constrained; the case
therefore stresses a singular-to-constrained transition.

All eight entries use eight MPI ranks, explicit cold starts, CG, nonlinear
tolerance $10^{-11}$, linear tolerance $10^{-14}$, and a 10000-iteration
nonlinear cap.  Hypre AMG uses a 600-iteration CG cap, and Hypre Jacobi uses a
60000-iteration cap.  Both BSR SpMV and assembled BSR are disabled.  Every
entry writes the initial field state and each accepted increment.

\section*{Completion and Timing}

Only the baseline and safeguarded-subspace trust-region methods with Hypre
Jacobi complete all 16 increments.  Both Newton line-search entries time out
without an accepted increment, and both PETSc Newton critical-point entries
fail on the first increment.  Baseline trust region with AMG accepts seven
increments and fails on increment 8; subspace trust region with AMG accepts 11
and fails on increment 12.  Every stored response state has a corresponding
visualization cycle.

returns.  The Newton-LS values are the outer 1800-second time limits because

Among the completed entries, subspace refinement reduces the reported
nonlinear work by 18.9\% and wall time by 12.0\% relative to baseline trust
region.  With AMG it advances four increments farther but still reaches the
nonlinear cap, so the added progress costs more time and does not change the
final outcome.  These are single-run measurements on a strongly
preconditioner-sensitive problem and do not establish a general performance
advantage.

\section*{Response Validation}

All accepted trust-region histories coincide at the precision written to CSV.
At the last state shared by all four trust-region entries, time is 0.4375, the
signed load-patch drive displacement is $-0.0150369$, and the applied load
resultant is 0.0115972.  The two completed Jacobi histories remain coincident
through time one and finish at signed drive displacement $-0.0210414$ and load
resultant 0.0265078.  Both completed entries contain 17 finite response states
and 17 field cycles.

The common partial path shows that the AMG failures do not precede a distinct
accepted branch in the recorded response quantity.  The comparison instead
isolates strong sensitivity of the nonlinear work to preconditioning during the
singular-to-constrained transition.

\section*{Solver Diagnostics}

Both PETSc Newton-CP entries report convergence reason $-4$ on the first
increment after their inner CG solve reaches its configured cap.  Newton-LS
encounters indefinite tangents repeatedly---123 diagnostics with AMG and 53
with Jacobi---then reports a nonfinite initial residual without producing an
accepted state.  The outer jobs are retained as timeouts because they do not
terminate before the imposed limit.

The AMG trust-region failures have a different signature.  No indefinite-CG
diagnostic or inner CG cap is reported, but the nonlinear solve reaches 10000
iterations with residual $1.85\times10^{-11}$ on increment 8 for baseline
trust region and $3.04\times10^{-11}$ on increment 12 for subspace trust
region.  Both residuals remain above the requested $10^{-11}$ tolerance.  With
Jacobi, the largest per-increment nonlinear counts are 308 and 286,
respectively, and both schedules complete.

\section*{Interpretation and Limitations}

This matrix supports a narrow robustness conclusion: under the common solver
contract, the two optimization-based trust-region configurations with Jacobi
are the only entries that complete the singular-to-constrained loading path.
It also shows that preconditioner choice can dominate the result; trust-region
globalization does not make the AMG configurations complete.

The case does not certify stability of the accepted states, validate a contact
formulation, or establish penetration accuracy.  It has no analytic reference,
accepted-state eigenanalysis, contact-gap study, load-step study, or penalty
sensitivity study.  It should therefore appear in the paper only as a
contact-like robustness and conditioning example, not as evidence for contact
accuracy or as the primary stable-equilibrium result.
