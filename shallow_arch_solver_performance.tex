\section*{Configuration}

The two-dimensional arch has span 12, thickness 0.35, rise 0.8, Young's modulus
10, and Poisson ratio 0.33.  It uses first-order displacement elements, 16 MPI
ranks, and the established 12288-element refined mesh.  The left support is
fixed while the right support moves left by 0.02 over 10 increments.  This
horizontal precompression is then held fixed while a spatially uniform downward
traction on the full top boundary ramps to 0.015 over 200 increments.  Warm
starts follow the accepted response path.  The response coordinate is the
average vertical displacement of the loaded top boundary, not a point
displacement at the crown.  The nonlinear and linear tolerances are
$10^{-11}$ and $10^{-14}$.

The production matrices record force--displacement histories without per-step
field output.  Hypre AMG uses a 600-iteration CG cap and Hypre Jacobi uses a
60000-iteration cap.  The comparison contains Newton-LS, Newton-CP, baseline
trust region, and safeguarded subspace trust region with both preconditioners.

\section*{Resolved Snap-Through}

The histories include several pre-transition states and a clear force-controlled
jump.  Between consecutive states the average top-boundary displacement changes from
$-0.069098$ to $-0.744258$ while applied force increases from 0.00819464 to
0.00910515.  The jump magnitude is 0.67516.  Every completed run ends at average
top-boundary displacement $-1.54095$, applied force 0.182103, and support reaction 0.182103.
The maximum force-balance discrepancy is below $1.7\times10^{-11}$.

Newton-LS/Jacobi crosses the jump on the same path as the trust-region runs.
Newton-LS/AMG reaches the transition and repeatedly reports a
non-positive-definite tangent, while both Newton-CP runs fail at the same
pre-jump state.  The result demonstrates a preconditioner-dependent robustness
advantage rather than a categorical inability of Newton methods to cross the
limit point.

\section*{Figures and Limitations}

The force--displacement histories are authoritative and are plotted using
positive downward displacement and force magnitudes.  Crosses mark the three
nonlinear-failure endpoints.  The current matrices
intentionally omitted per-step fields, so the earlier deformation sequence is
not reused for this redesigned problem.

The force-controlled calculation records the physical jump between stable
branches but does not trace the unstable descending branch.  Tracing that branch
would require displacement or arc-length control and is outside this comparison.
