\section*{Shallow-Arch Solver Performance}

This report summarizes the current-code shallow-arch response matrix run on
\texttt{rzwhippet23} on 2026-08-17.  The authoritative full-output matrix is
\path{build-rzwhippet-toss_4_x86_64_ib-llvm@19.1.3-release/paper_shallow_arch_response_20260817_171856}.
Commands, logs, response histories, executable and source identity, status, and
validation tables are archived under
\path{/usr/workspace/tupek2/dev/buckling_solver_paper/results/shallow_arch/solver_response_20260817_171856}.

\section*{Configuration}

The two-dimensional arch has span 12, thickness 0.35, rise 0.8, Young's modulus
10, and Poisson ratio 0.33.  It uses first-order displacement elements, 16 MPI
ranks, the established 12288-element refined small mesh, explicit cold
starts, and 20 equal load increments.  The vertical traction magnitude reaches
0.015, corresponding to total applied force 0.182103 on the top boundary.  Every
run writes ParaView output for the initial state and every accepted increment.

The nonlinear tolerance is $10^{-11}$ and the linear tolerance is $10^{-14}$.
Both preconditioners use CG.  Hypre AMG has a 600-iteration cap, and Hypre Jacobi
has a 60000-iteration cap.  The eight configurations combine Newton line search,
PETSc Newton critical-point line search, baseline trust region, and safeguarded
trust-region subspace globalization with both preconditioners.

\section*{Completion and Timing}

All eight configurations completed all 20 increments with exit status zero.  Each
run contains 21 finite response states and 21 visualization cycles.  Times below
are challenge-suite max-rank wall times; the iteration column is the sum of
reported nonlinear iterations over all increments.

Hypre AMG reduces wall time by factors of 5.9--7.6 relative to Hypre Jacobi.
The subspace trust-region/AMG configuration is fastest at 4.161 seconds, 3.5\%
below baseline trust region.  With Jacobi, baseline trust region is fastest and
the subspace path adds 1.4\%.  Newton-CP remains close to both trust-region paths.
By contrast, cold-start Newton-LS requires 3691 reported iterations, including
1884 on the first increment, and costs 29.6 times the baseline trust-region time
with AMG and 37.6 times with Jacobi.

\section*{Response Validation}

All eight force--displacement and reaction histories are identical at the precision
written to CSV.  They begin at zero load, displacement, and reaction and end at
average crown displacement $-3.34148$, applied force 0.182103, and support reaction
0.182103.  The equality of applied force and support reaction at every accepted
state confirms the corrected global reaction calculation for this case.

The first accepted increment already reaches crown displacement $-0.962872$, whose
magnitude exceeds the initial rise of 0.8.  Thus the 20-step load schedule crosses
the inversion event in its first increment.  This matrix is a useful comparison of
nonlinear robustness, post-snap response, and preconditioner cost, but it does not
resolve the critical load or a pre-snap limit point.  A dedicated critical-load
study would require refined loading near the first increment.

\section*{Solver Diagnostics}

No entry reports a nonlinear failure, timeout, nonfinite state, or missing field
cycle.  The response agreement shows that the timing spread is not caused by
different accepted branches.  The high Newton-LS work instead reflects the cost of
re-solving the difficult first-increment path from a zero increment at every load
step.  This matrix does not include accepted-state eigenvalue calculations and
therefore does not establish tangent definiteness or certify stability.
